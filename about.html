My main focus is GPU compiler and driver code. More broadly, I work on compilers and operating systems.

now

Most days, I end up somewhere between Mesa, LLVM, Linux DRM, and Wayland. I tend to follow whatever question has caught my attention across the stack, and it is easy for me to lose track of time while reading code, tracing behavior, or following an idea all the way down to the hardware.

I’m looking for GPU compiler or driver work where that curiosity is useful: understanding hardware deeply, reasoning about its performance limits, and pushing real workloads closer to them.

systems background

My systems background includes NuttX bring-up on RK3588S and TI AM67A. I upstreamed the initial Orange Pi 5 Pro port; at Baykar, I brought up a Cortex-R5F through Linux remoteproc.

background

The short version: I kept opening black boxes one layer further down.

More

Before I knew the terms debugger or reverse engineering, I modified small Visual Basic programs and used Cheat Engine to inspect game state. I also ran Minecraft servers, rooted phones, installed custom ROMs, and poked around router interfaces.

I started writing code regularly in high school in 2017, making small games with friends, learning C# and Java, and taking part in Codeforces contests. I discovered Linux around 2019, then moved into graphics programming in 2022, building small OpenGL and Vulkan renderers.

Later, curiosity about how computers actually work drew me toward operating systems and computer architecture. Wanting to understand where performance comes from led me to compilers and microarchitecture.
